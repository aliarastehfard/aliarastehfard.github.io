### Identity in Context
I remember that growing up I always enjoyed acting. My parents signed me and my sister up in theatrical acting class, and we loved it. Admittedly though, my sister was better at it than I was. To me, it felt genuine because people knew that I was acting. They would teach us how to walk, how to confidently walk up the stairs, etc. I remember that me and my sister quickly were promoted to take the class with the senior students in the class (more experience ones) although we had been in it for only a few weeks. It was the first time I felt genuine and free because I was given the permission to pretend to be someone other than myself and not feel guilty about it, and when our parents pulled us out -- worried, I think, about our futures and our schoolwork -- I felt wrecked. This would mean that I had to go back to that daily life of being "me" who was not popular at all. He would try so hard to fit in a group and be recognized.

But we didn't stop there. Acting for us was more than just the class. We would use our mother's old Samsung cell phone, plot scenarios, and record ourselves playing it. It felt as if we could be whoever we wanted to. No fear of judgement.
